# Prototype Testing & Iteration

## 1. Prototype Objective

The first LETS prototype was developed to test whether recycled paper could be used as the primary pencil-body material without significantly changing the everyday user experience.

The prototype focused on validating two fundamental product requirements:

1. Writing functionality
2. Standard sharpening compatibility

---

## 2. Prototype

The prototype consists of:

* Recycled-paper pencil body
* Graphite writing core
* Exterior finishing
* LETS branding

![LETS Prototype](../assets/lets-prototype.jpg)

*LETS recycled-paper pencil prototype.*

---

## 3. Prototype Testing

### Test 1 — Writing Performance

**Objective:**
Determine whether the recycled-paper pencil provides a usable writing experience comparable to a conventional pencil.

**Result:**
The prototype was able to provide a writing experience comparable to a conventional wooden pencil.

**Product implication:**
The recycled-paper body can potentially replace the conventional wooden body without changing the fundamental writing use case.

---

### Test 2 — Sharpening Compatibility

**Objective:**
Determine whether customers would need a special sharpener.

**Result:**
The prototype could be sharpened using a regular pencil sharpener.

**Product implication:**
Users can continue using an existing accessory, reducing friction during adoption.

---

## 5. Key Product Learning

The most important learning from the prototype was:

> **Sustainability does not need to require a change in the customer's basic pencil usage.**

The prototype demonstrated that the product can retain two familiar behaviors:

**Write normally → Sharpen normally**

while changing the underlying pencil-body material.

---

## 6. Remaining Product Risks

The prototype validates the basic concept but does not yet validate commercial-scale production.

Key risks requiring further testing include:

### Manufacturing Consistency

Can paper rolling, lead integration and finishing produce consistent pencils at higher volumes?

### Durability

Can the recycled-paper body withstand prolonged everyday use?

### Cost at Scale

Can the current prototype cost of ₹1.50 per pencil be maintained or reduced after accounting for machinery, labour, packaging and wastage?

### Quality Control

Can automated or semi-automated production maintain consistent dimensions, lead alignment and finishing?

---

## 7. Next Prototype Iteration

The next iteration should focus on:

1. Improving paper-body consistency.
2. Improving lead alignment.
3. Testing durability over extended use.
4. Standardizing the paper-rolling process.
5. Testing sharpening performance across multiple samples.
6. Evaluating machine-assisted production.
7. Measuring production time per pencil.

---

## 8. Product Development Loop

The planned development cycle is:

**Build → Test → Learn → Improve → Retest**

The current prototype represents the first validation cycle. Future iterations will focus increasingly on manufacturing consistency, cost optimization and scalability.

---

## 9. PM Takeaway

The prototype stage reduced uncertainty around the core product concept while identifying the next major risks: **scalability, consistency, durability and unit economics**.

This shifts the next product-development priority from simply proving that the pencil works to proving that it can be **manufactured consistently and economically at scale**.
