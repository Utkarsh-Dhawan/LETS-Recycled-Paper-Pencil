# Go-to-Market Strategy

## 1. GTM Objective

The objective of the LETS go-to-market strategy is to introduce the recycled-paper pencil through education and stationery channels before expanding toward broader consumer and institutional markets.

The strategy is designed around:

> **Accessible pricing + existing distribution channels + institutional adoption**

---

## 2. Initial Target Market

### Primary Segment

**Educational Market**

* School students
* College students
* Schools
* Educational institutions

This segment is prioritized because pencils are already a familiar stationery product and educational institutions can generate both individual and bulk demand.

---

## 3. Distribution Model

The initial distribution model is:

**LETS → Wholesaler → Retailer → Customer**

### LETS

Manufactures the recycled-paper pencil and sells to wholesalers.

**Current proposed wholesale price: ₹3.00/pencil**

### Wholesaler

Purchases LETS pencils in bulk and distributes them to stationery retailers.

### Retailer

Makes LETS available to end customers through stationery stores and educational supply channels.

**Current proposed retailer purchase price: ₹3.80/pencil**

### Customer

Students, parents and other stationery users purchase the product for everyday use.

---

## 4. Market Entry Strategy

### Phase 1 — Pilot

Focus on a limited geographic/educational market.

Objectives:

* Test customer acceptance
* Validate product quality
* Test pricing
* Identify manufacturing issues
* Gather retailer feedback
* Understand repeat-purchase potential

The pilot should begin with a manageable number of retailers and institutional customers rather than attempting immediate mass distribution.

---

## 5. Phase 2 — Retail Expansion

After validating the product:

**Increase wholesaler partnerships → Increase retailer coverage → Increase customer availability**

Potential channels:

* Local stationery wholesalers
* Stationery retailers
* Educational supply stores
* College/school stationery outlets

The objective is to make LETS available through channels customers already use to purchase pencils.

---

## 6. Phase 3 — Institutional Sales

LETS can develop a dedicated B2B offering for:

* Schools
* Colleges
* Educational organizations
* Events
* Sustainability initiatives

### Institutional Value Proposition

> **Affordable sustainable stationery that can be customized for institutional use.**

Potential customization:

* Institution logo
* Event branding
* Custom colors/designs
* Bulk packaging

---

## 7. Phase 4 — Broader Consumer Market

Once product quality, manufacturing consistency and distribution economics are validated, LETS can expand toward:

* Parents
* General consumers
* Online stationery buyers
* Sustainable-product consumers

The long-term objective is to transition LETS from an educational product into a broader sustainable stationery brand.

### Sales

* Units sold
* Units sold per retailer
* Wholesale order size
* Institutional order volume

### Product

* Product complaints
* Replacement/defect rate
* Customer feedback
* Repeat purchases

### Business

* Gross margin
* Cost per pencil
* Customer acquisition cost
* Contribution margin

---

## 11. GTM Risks

### Risk 1 — Customer Switching

Customers may not see enough benefit to switch from conventional pencils.

**Mitigation:** Emphasize familiar functionality alongside sustainability.

### Risk 2 — Retailer Adoption

Retailers may hesitate to introduce an unfamiliar product.

**Mitigation:** Start with a small pilot and demonstrate product quality and economics.

### Risk 3 — Manufacturing Capacity

Demand may grow faster than production capability.

**Mitigation:** Scale manufacturing gradually and mechanize repetitive processes.

### Risk 4 — Cost Increase at Scale

Commercial costs may exceed prototype-level costs.

**Mitigation:** Track unit economics continuously and optimize production before large-scale expansion.

---

## 12. GTM Strategy Summary

LETS will initially enter the market through **educational and stationery channels**, using wholesalers and retailers to reach end customers.

The growth path is:

> **Educational Pilot → Retail Distribution → Institutional Sales → Customized Orders → Mass-Market Expansion**

The strategy prioritizes proving product-market fit and unit economics before committing to large-scale expansion.

---

## 13. PM Takeaway

The GTM strategy is designed around a simple principle:

> **Use existing customer behavior and distribution infrastructure rather than asking the market to adopt an entirely new buying habit.**

LETS is still a pencil. The innovation is in **what it is made from, how it is manufactured, and how sustainably it can be scaled.**
